# Energy Zone Design Guidelines

Swimming Canada AAD/LTAD Framework — Set Design Reference for Velocity (T2T)

This file is a design reference. Zone volume proportions are contextual guidance,
not hard session constraints — the app does not track cumulative weekly volume by zone.
Apply zone logic to set structure, work:rest ratios, and intensity selection.

---

## Zone A-I — Aerobic Base / Easy

### Purpose
Oxidative enzyme activity, capillary density, cardiac stroke volume, and technical
efficiency. Neuromuscular skill patterns are encoded here through volume and repetition.
Efficiency at low speed must be established before it can be maintained under load.

### Effort markers
- HR: < 130–159 bpm; ~60–70% HR max
- RPE: 1–3 (very easy → moderate)
- Blood lactate: < 2 mmol/L
- Intensity descriptor: **easy**

### Seasonal proportion (reference only)
GPP ~40–50% of total volume; decreases as phase progresses.

### Set design principles
1. Prioritize continuity and duration over short-interval fragmentation
2. Technical load is the primary purpose — assign a specific focus per set, not just "swim easy"
3. Monitor stroke count and underwater distance; creep signals drift into A-II
4. Equipment fits well here: fins extend UW distance, snorkel removes breathing disruption
5. Even splits required; negative splitting acceptable; descending splits are a red flag

### Work:rest
- Continuous: 1:0
- Extensive interval: 1:0.1 to 1:0.2 (10–15 sec rest per 100m)
- Drill sets: up to 1:0.5 to protect execution quality

### Volume parameters
Set distance: 800–4,000m depending on phase. Duration: 15–40+ minutes.

### Common errors
- Drifting pace: swimmers gradually accelerate, leaving A-I
- No technical anchor: "easy swimming" without a focus produces poor muscle memory
- Skipping A-I in SPP/Comp: aerobic base is "use it or lose it" — 2–3 A-I sessions/week minimum

## Zone A-II — Aerobic Development / Moderate

### Purpose
VO2 development, mitochondrial density, cardiac output, capacity to sustain high
aerobic output over time. The workhorse zone for 400m–1500m swimmers and the
platform for all threshold work. Blood lactate elevated but stable (~2–4 mmol/L).

### Effort markers
- HR: 160–185 bpm; ~70–80% HR max
- RPE: 3–5 (somewhat hard → hard)
- Blood lactate: 2–4 mmol/L (elevated, stable)
- Intensity descriptor: **moderate** to **strong**

### Seasonal proportion (reference only)
GPP ~25–35% of total volume; primary growth zone in GPP.

### Set design principles
1. Short rest (10–30 sec per 100m) keeps HR elevated across the full set
2. Even splits across reps are mandatory — descending (going faster) signals pace was too easy
3. Descending distance construction (300–200–100) builds pace awareness
4. Goal is time in zone, not peak pace — if swimmers chase interval times into R-III, sendoff is too tight
5. When stacking zones in one session, place higher-intensity work first; A-II after sprint still produces the aerobic adaptation

### Work:rest
- Short rest (aerobic emphasis): 1:0.1 to 1:0.3
- Medium rest: 1:0.3 to 1:0.5
- 1:2 at the same pace shifts toward anaerobic — avoid in pure A-II

### Volume parameters
Set distance: 1,000–4,000m. Rep distance: 100–400m preferred. Duration: 15–40 minutes.

### Common errors
- Tight sendoffs producing R-III work: looks like A-II but HR and pace are threshold
- Too much rest: swimmers fully recover each rep, losing aerobic accumulation
- No pace accountability: without even-split enforcement, swimmers drift fast then fade

## Zone R-III — Race Pace / Threshold

### Purpose
Race-pace conditioning, lactate tolerance, race-specific technical proficiency.
Both aerobic and anaerobic systems contribute significantly. This zone simulates
competition demands and teaches the body to sustain high-quality output under
metabolic stress. Blood lactate: 4–8 mmol/L.

### Effort markers
- HR: 185+ bpm; 90–100% HR max
- RPE: 6–8 (very hard)
- Blood lactate: 4–8 mmol/L
- Intensity descriptor: **strong** to **fast**

### Seasonal proportion (reference only)
GPP ~16%; SPP ~25%; Comp ~27%.

### Set design principles
1. Broken sets designed to simulate race speed under managed fatigue
2. Fartlek structures are valuable — variable effort within a longer swim allows R-III spikes in aerobic context
3. Rest must be sufficient to maintain quality; significant split degradation within reps means pace is too fast or rest too short
4. Session sequencing: highest intensity first when the goal is speed; aerobic first when goal is aerobic development under fatigue
5. Race-distance anchoring: threshold pace means different things for different events — for a generalist group, anchor to effort quality, not specific event pace

### Work:rest
- Threshold (lower R-III): 1:0.2 to 1:0.5
- Race pace: 1:0.5 to 1:1.5
- 1:2 at this intensity shifts toward anaerobic — useful occasionally but not standard

### Volume parameters
Set distance: 400–2,000m. Rep distance: 50–400m. Duration: 6–30 minutes.
Sessions: 1–3 per week depending on phase.

### Common errors
- Disguised A-II: sendoffs that feel hard but don't actually stress the threshold
- Too short rest with too fast a target: swimmers can't hit race pace after rep 3
- Ignoring technical collapse: allowing form breakdown at race pace teaches poor neuromuscular patterns at exactly the speeds that matter

## Zone S-IV — Speed Endurance / Above Threshold

### Purpose
Glycolytic enzyme activation, buffering capacity, ability to maintain speed under
severe metabolic acidosis. Develops capacity to sustain speed in the 100m–200m range.
Also builds neuromuscular patterns for high-speed stroke mechanics under fatigue.
Blood lactate: 8–14+ mmol/L. Total work volume is deliberately low.

### Effort markers
- HR: max or near-max (above R-III ceiling)
- RPE: 8–9 (very hard to near-maximal)
- Blood lactate: 8–14+ mmol/L
- Intensity descriptor: **sprint**

### Seasonal proportion (reference only)
S-IV + S-V combined: ~6% across all phases. This ceiling is consistent — quality drives adaptation, not volume.

### Set design principles
1. Short repetitions, generous rest — insufficient rest converts S-IV to a poor R-III set
2. Race-specific mechanics under near-maximal output is the point; specify which technical elements to protect
3. Total work per set is capped — accumulating 500m+ at "race-pace-ish" effort is not S-IV
4. Swimmer should be able to describe their effort: "that was 95%"
5. This zone is purposeful and occasional — not a default filler

### Work:rest
1:4 to 1:6 minimum. Passive rest preferred. 1:2 at this intensity compromises quality.

### Volume parameters
Total work per set: 25–200m. Rep distance: 10–50m. Duration: 2–8 min work, 10–20 min total.
Sessions: 1–2 per week in SPP/Comp; 1 or less in GPP.

### Common errors
- Insufficient rest: the most common error; converts S-IV to fatiguing R-III
- Too much volume: length does not drive the adaptation — quality does
- Using S-IV as a default filler: this zone requires fresh legs and a session designed around it

## Zone S-V — Sprint / Maximum Velocity

**Deprioritized in standard SetForge session design. Include only when explicitly requested.**

### Purpose
ATP-CP (phosphagen) system. Maximum neurological and muscular speed in very short
durations (≤ 10 seconds). Develops explosive starts, breakout speed, and peak velocity.
Neural recruitment patterns cannot be developed at any lower intensity.

### Effort markers
- HR: not a reliable marker at this duration
- RPE: 10 (maximal)
- Efforts: ≤ 10 seconds
- Intensity descriptor: **all out**

### Set design principles
1. Maximum effort is non-negotiable — 90% effort teaches 90% of maximum speed
2. Complete rest required: 2–5 minutes for full phosphagen recovery
3. Session placement: S-V work at the start of the main set, not at the end of a fatiguing session
4. 6–12 total efforts per session maximum — adaptation is neural, not metabolic
5. Starts, walls, and underwater phases are the primary vehicles

### Work:rest
1:8 to 1:20. Passive rest only — any active swimming between reps reduces phosphagen recovery.

### Volume parameters
Work per effort: 6–25m (≤ 10 seconds). Total efforts: 4–12 per session.

### Common errors
- Too little rest: turns S-V into a lactate tolerance session
- Sub-maximum effort: "comfortable hard" does not develop sprint speed
- Too many reps: volume instinct overrides the neural adaptation logic

## Cross-Zone Principles

### The 6% ceiling
S-IV and S-V combined should not exceed 6% of water volume in any phase.
This is not a soft guideline — exceeding it without compensating A-I/A-II volume
risks erosion of the aerobic base that all speed work depends upon.

### Work:rest is the primary design lever
At the same intensity, work:rest ratio determines whether a set is aerobic or anaerobic
in emphasis. The coach does not prescribe pace alone — the rest interval is what
targets the adaptation.

### Session sequencing
When combining zones in one session: highest-intensity work first while the nervous
system is fresh. Exception: a moderate aerobic primer before R-III/S-IV in SPP/Comp
is valid — but S-V work always comes before aerobic volume.

### Polarized loading
Most volume at A-I/A-II (low intensity), meaningful volume at R-III/S-IV (high intensity),
very little in the moderate-to-threshold middle. The "moderate-intensity trap" — most
work at a pace too fast for aerobic adaptation but too slow for threshold adaptation —
is one of the most common errors in competitive swimming programming.

### Technical quality across all zones
Every zone has a technical variable worth monitoring:
- A-I: stroke count, underwater distance
- A-II: even splits, stroke length under aerobic load
- R-III: stroke maintenance under threshold fatigue
- S-IV: race mechanics under metabolic stress
- S-V: maximum efficiency at maximum velocity
